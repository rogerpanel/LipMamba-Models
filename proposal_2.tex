\section{Executive Summary}
% ============================================================

Selective state-space models (SSMs) such as Mamba have emerged as efficient alternatives to transformers for language modeling, reducing quadratic attention to $\calO(L \log L)$ complexity. However, the January 2026 HiSPA paper revealed a \textbf{structurally unique vulnerability}: short trigger phrases can \textbf{irreversibly overwrite Mamba's recurrent hidden states}, causing partial amnesia---the model forgets safety instructions, authorization contexts, or prior conversation content. This ``hidden state poisoning'' has no transformer analog and threatens all SSM-based security-critical deployments.

This proposal addresses the \textbf{complete absence of certified robustness theory for SSM/Mamba architectures}. While Lipschitz certification has been scaled to transformers (LipShiFT, ICLR 2025; LipNeXt, arXiv 2601.18513), the \textbf{selective gating mechanism} and \textbf{structured state transition matrices} in Mamba require fundamentally new analysis. We propose \textbf{LipMamba}, which: (1) derives tight Lipschitz bounds for selective SSM layers including input-dependent discretization, (2) enforces these bounds during training via spectrally-constrained parameterization, (3) provides PAC-Bayesian generalization certificates extending the PI's MambaShield framework, and (4) proves certified immunity regions against hidden state poisoning attacks. Expected outcome: the first Mamba language model with provable robustness guarantees, maintaining $\geq$90\% of baseline perplexity while certifying robustness against state-poisoning triggers up to length 32.

\section{Problem Statement and Research Gap}
% ============================================================

\subsection{The Hidden State Poisoning Threat}

The HiSPA attack (arXiv 2601.01972) demonstrated that Mamba's recurrent structure creates a fundamentally different adversarial surface than transformers. In a transformer, each token attends to all prior tokens---a poisoned token cannot ``erase'' prior context because attention is recomputed at every layer. In Mamba, information flows through a compressed hidden state $\mathbf{h}_t \in \R^{N \times D}$ governed by:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_t = \bar{\mathbf{A}}_t \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \bar{\mathbf{B}}_t \mathbf{x}_t
\end{equation}

where $\bar{\mathbf{A}}_t = \exp(\Delta_t \mathbf{A})$ and $\bar{\mathbf{B}}_t = (\Delta_t \mathbf{A})^{-1}(\bar{\mathbf{A}}_t - \mathbf{I})\Delta_t \mathbf{B}_t$ are \textbf{input-dependent} (selective) discretizations. A crafted trigger can choose $\Delta_t$ and $\mathbf{B}_t$ values that drive $\bar{\mathbf{A}}_t \approx \mathbf{0}$, effectively zeroing the hidden state and erasing all prior context.

The ROBENCH-25 benchmark confirmed this is a \textbf{structural vulnerability of SSM recurrence}, not a training artifact. Security implications include: guardrail omission (the model ``forgets'' its safety system prompt), authorization-token forgetting, and redaction leaks.

\textbf{No certified robustness framework exists for selective SSMs.} The key technical obstacle is that the selectivity mechanism ($\Delta_t$, $\mathbf{B}_t$, $\mathbf{C}_t$ are all functions of $\mathbf{x}_t$) makes the state transition \textbf{input-dependent}, breaking the standard Lipschitz analysis for linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. The PI's MambaShield provided PAC-Bayesian generalization certificates and adversarial training for Mamba in the intrusion detection domain, but did not derive Lipschitz bounds for the selective gating mechanism itself.

\subsection{Why This Gap Matters}

As Mamba-based models are deployed for agentic tasks (tool calling, code execution, multi-turn conversation management), the hidden state poisoning vulnerability becomes a \textbf{critical safety risk}. Without certified bounds, there is no principled way to determine whether a given input sequence can trigger state collapse. This proposal fills this gap with the first complete Lipschitz-certified SSM framework.

\section{Research Objectives}
% ============================================================

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{O1:} Derive tight Lipschitz bounds for selective SSM layers, accounting for input-dependent discretization $(\Delta_t, \mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t)$.
    
    \item \textbf{O2:} Design spectrally-constrained parameterizations that enforce Lipschitz bounds during training without destroying selectivity.
    
    \item \textbf{O3:} Prove certified immunity regions against hidden state poisoning: given a known trigger length $\ell$, certify that no trigger of length $\leq \ell$ can reduce the hidden state norm below a safety threshold.
    
    \item \textbf{O4:} Extend PAC-Bayesian certificates (from MambaShield) to the Lipschitz-constrained architecture, providing simultaneous generalization and robustness guarantees.
    
    \item \textbf{O5:} Train and evaluate LipMamba language models at 130M, 370M, and 1.3B parameter scales, comparing perplexity-robustness trade-offs with transformer baselines.
    
    \item \textbf{O6:} Develop ROBENCH-26, an extended benchmark for SSM robustness including hidden state poisoning, adversarial suffixes, and distribution-shift scenarios.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed Methodology}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Phase 1: Lipschitz Analysis of Selective SSM (Months 1--6)}

\subsubsection{The Selectivity Challenge}

In a standard (non-selective) SSM, $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$ are fixed matrices, and the Lipschitz constant of the sequence-to-sequence map is bounded by the transfer function $\norm{\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{I} - z\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B}}_\infty$. In selective Mamba, these matrices depend on the input:

\begin{align}
    \mathbf{B}_t &= \text{Linear}_B(\mathbf{x}_t) \in \R^{N} \\
    \mathbf{C}_t &= \text{Linear}_C(\mathbf{x}_t) \in \R^{N} \\
    \Delta_t &= \text{softplus}(\text{Linear}_\Delta(\mathbf{x}_t)) \in \R^{D}_{>0}
\end{align}

This makes the system a \textbf{nonlinear dynamical system} rather than a linear one.

\subsubsection{Proposed Bound}

\begin{theorem}[Lipschitz Bound for Selective SSM Layer]
Let $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, \ldots, x_L)$ and $\mathbf{x}' = (x_1', \ldots, x_L')$ be two input sequences. Define:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $L_B, L_C, L_\Delta$: Lipschitz constants of the projection networks,
    \item $\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A})$: largest eigenvalue of the (diagonal, negative) state matrix,
    \item $\Delta_{\max} = \max_t \Delta_t$: maximum step size.
\end{itemize}
Then the output map $\mathbf{y} = \text{SSM}(\mathbf{x})$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{y}'}_2 \leq \left(\underbrace{L_C \cdot \norm{\mathbf{h}}_\infty}_{\text{readout}} + \underbrace{\norm{\mathbf{C}}_\infty \cdot \Gamma(L_B, L_\Delta, \lambda_{\max}, L)}_{\text{state dynamics}}\right) \cdot \norm{\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}'}_2
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma$ is a recursively-computable bound on the state sensitivity that accounts for the input-dependent discretization.
\end{theorem}

The key insight is to decompose the sensitivity into: (a) the effect of input perturbation on the current time step's selectivity parameters, and (b) the propagation of state perturbation through subsequent time steps. The second component is bounded by the spectral radius of $\bar{\mathbf{A}}_t$, which we constrain.

\subsubsection{Spectral Constraint Enforcement}

To enforce the Lipschitz bound during training, we parameterize:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{State matrix $\mathbf{A}$:} Constrain eigenvalues to $\lambda_i \in [-\lambda_{\max}, -\lambda_{\min}]$ via reparameterization $\mathbf{A} = -\text{diag}(\lambda_{\min} + (\lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}) \cdot \sigma(\boldsymbol{\alpha}))$.
    
    \item \textbf{Projection networks:} Apply spectral normalization to $\mathbf{W}_B$, $\mathbf{W}_C$, $\mathbf{W}_\Delta$ with target spectral norms $s_B$, $s_C$, $s_\Delta$.
    
    \item \textbf{Step size $\Delta_t$:} Clip $\Delta_t \leq \Delta_{\max}$ via a saturating activation replacing softplus.
\end{enumerate}

The total layer Lipschitz constant is then a computable function of $(s_B, s_C, s_\Delta, \lambda_{\max}, \lambda_{\min}, \Delta_{\max}, L)$.

\subsection{Phase 2: Certified Hidden State Poisoning Immunity (Months 4--10)}

\begin{definition}[Hidden State Poisoning]
A trigger sequence $\boldsymbol{\tau} = (\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_\ell)$ at position $t_0$ is an $(\alpha, \ell)$-poisoning attack if:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\norm{\mathbf{h}_{t_0+\ell}^{\text{poisoned}}}}{\norm{\mathbf{h}_{t_0}^{\text{clean}}}} \leq \alpha
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \ll 1$ indicates near-complete state erasure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Certified Poisoning Immunity]
Under LipMamba's spectral constraints, if $\rho(\bar{\mathbf{A}}_t) \geq \rho_{\min} > 0$ for all valid inputs and the input norm is bounded by $\norm{\mathbf{x}_t} \leq X_{\max}$, then for any trigger of length $\ell$:
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\mathbf{h}_{t_0+\ell}} \geq \rho_{\min}^\ell \cdot \norm{\mathbf{h}_{t_0}} - \frac{\bar{B}_{\max} \cdot X_{\max} \cdot (1 - \rho_{\min}^\ell)}{1 - \rho_{\min}}
\end{equation}
This provides a lower bound on the post-trigger state norm, certifying that the hidden state cannot be driven below a threshold.
\end{theorem}

The certified immunity radius is: for a given minimum acceptable state retention ratio $\alpha_{\min}$, the maximum trigger length $\ell^*$ satisfying the bound determines the certification scope.

\subsection{Phase 3: PAC-Bayesian Integration (Months 8--14)}

Extend the PI's MambaShield PAC-Bayes framework. For a posterior $Q$ over Lipschitz-constrained SSM parameters:

\begin{equation}
    \E_{\mathbf{w} \sim Q}\left[\calL_{\text{adv}}(\mathbf{w})\right] \leq \hat{\calL}_{\text{adv}}^S(\mathbf{w}) + \sqrt{\frac{\text{KL}(Q \| P) + \ln(2n/\delta)}{2n}} + \underbrace{\frac{L_{\text{SSM}} \cdot \epsilon}{2}}_{\text{robustness penalty}}
\end{equation}

where $L_{\text{SSM}}$ is the certified Lipschitz constant of the full model and $\epsilon$ is the perturbation radius. This provides a \textbf{simultaneous} generalization and robustness guarantee.

\section{Significance}
% ============================================================

This work would establish the \textbf{theoretical foundation for certified robustness of state-space model language architectures}---a gap that, if left unfilled, will become a critical liability as Mamba-class models proliferate in production. The combination of Lipschitz analysis, PAC-Bayesian certificates, and certified poisoning immunity would create the SSM analog of what LipShiFT achieved for transformers, but with the additional dimension of hidden state protection that is unique to recurrent architectures.
